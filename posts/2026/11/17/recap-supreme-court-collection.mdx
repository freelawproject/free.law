## Advanced Full-Text Search and Docket and Query Alerts Let Anyone Follow the Nation’s Highest Court

OAKLAND, CA – Free Law Project today launched a free, searchable archive of Supreme Court filings dating back to 2000, with email alerts for new filings. Going beyond the case law Free Law Project already archives, the new collection includes the briefs, motions, orders, and other filings that offer crucial insights into how justice unfolds in the United States. Researchers, journalists, and the public can now search the full text of these documents and sign up for notifications about updates in the cases and topics they care about most.

“Democratizing the law means building tools that help the public understand what’s happening in the cases that matter,” said Free Law Project Executive Director Michael Lissner. “Our tools provide unprecedented methods for analyzing both the arguments made to the Supreme Court and how the Court responds.”

The Supreme Court collection covers over a million docket entries from hundreds of thousands of court cases, and new filings are added to the collection within minutes of being registered in the Supreme Court’s electronic filing system. The collection is part of the [RECAP Archive][recap-archive], the free and searchable library of legal filings, decisions, and case materials that Free Law Project maintains.

The Supreme Court has long provided some mechanisms for public access to its docket. For example, the opinions of the Supreme Court dating back to the 1700s are [published online][us-reports] in PDF form. But its docket search is limited: visitors to the Court’s website can look up cases by name or party, but they can’t search the text of the filings and opinions.

Free Law Project’s search tool lets users find any word or phrase in any Supreme Court case since 2000, whether it appears in an amicus brief, a motion, or an opinion or order from the Court. The collection also supports advanced [search and query][advanced-search] techniques—allowing researchers to narrow results to specific date ranges, exclude terms, and find words that appear near each other. A reporter could, for example, pull up every brief filed between 2019 and 2020 that mentions “open internet” or could compare how many times amicus briefs used the phrase “national security” in 2000 versus 2002.

Users can set docket alerts to receive an email update when there is a new entry in a case they follow. [Search alerts][search-alerts] go further: users can save a query, such as new amicus briefs that mention “fair use,” and then receive an email whenever a new filing matches.

“Researchers trying to follow these court cases shouldn’t be forced to continuously refresh a browser to see if the Supreme Court has issued a new opinion or a special interest group has submitted an amicus brief,” said Lissner.

Free Law Project is grateful to [GlassCanon][glasscanon], a platform for legal intelligence and analysis, for funding this project.

The Supreme Court collection lives in Free Law Project’s RECAP Archive. Since 2009, the [RECAP browser extension][recap] has liberated public court records from the government-run Public Access to Court Electronic Records (PACER) system and added them to the RECAP Archive. This ensures free access to millions of court documents from the federal courts, most of which would otherwise be locked behind a paywall. The Supreme Court collection is the first non-PACER data to be integrated into the RECAP Archive, but it won’t be the last: Free Law Project is currently working to add state court dockets and expects the first states to go online in 2027.

As a nonprofit organization, Free Law Project proudly makes all of our data freely available to everyone. Members who donate to Free Law Project can support our work in these projects as well as get advanced alert features and more frequent API requests. [Learn more about membership.][membership]

## Upcoming

**AI Connector Integration:** In the coming weeks, we will be adding this Supreme Court collection to our [connector for AI assistants][ai-connector].

**Webhook support:** The initial launch of the Supreme Court collection includes Docket Alerts and Search Alerts. Later, we’ll add API access to SCOTUS content and webhook support for docket and search alerts.

**State Court Dockets:** We are currently working to add state court dockets and expect the first states to go online in 2027.

**Expanding the Supreme Court collection:** We are also seeking support to import the scanned Supreme Court records and briefs collection, which is comprised of the [briefs and transcripts of records][loc] (or joint appendices) submitted to the Supreme Court of the United States on appeal from federal courts and state appellate courts.

## About Free Law Project

Free Law Project (FLP) is a nonprofit organization dedicated to making the legal system more transparent, accessible, and equitable through technology. Best known for its flagship platform, CourtListener, Free Law Project provides free public access to millions of court opinions, filings, and legal data, all of which serve researchers, journalists, attorneys, and the public.

The organization builds and maintains open-source tools, APIs, and data services that improve the transparency of the legal system and power innovation across the legal ecosystem. Its work empowers legal tech startups, supports investigative journalists, and enables developers, academic researchers, and legal organizations to access and use high-quality legal data to better understand and analyze the law.

Through Justice Initiatives, Free Law Project develops and deploys tools that directly address the access to justice crisis. These efforts focus on helping people better understand and navigate the legal system, reducing barriers to participation, and improving outcomes for those who would otherwise face the system alone. Alongside this work, Free Law Project advances advocacy efforts that promote open courts, public access to legal information, and a more equitable justice system.

## About GlassCanon

GlassCanon is a platform for providing advanced legal analytics on large scale litigation data in the United States. Currently it serves cutting edge graph analytics and litigation trend detection, as well as providing custom datasets (built from CourtListener data) and code to the legal analysis community.

Inspired by open source values, and the lack of easily accessible bulk legal data, it is making its initial data and analytic code base available upon request to scholars, practitioners, and the public interest community. GlassCanon is dedicated to providing cutting edge empirical methods for both academia and industry. It believes that as data is integrated more into practice the legal community should embrace and explore a wider toolset to understand and navigate the increasingly industrialized legal landscape.
